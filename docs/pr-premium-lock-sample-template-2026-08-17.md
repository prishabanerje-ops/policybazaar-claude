**PRESS RELEASE**
**For immediate release**
**Dubai, United Arab Emirates — [DATE]**

## Headline

**Two months on, UAE residents are locking health insurance premiums for five years, and Policybazaar.ae says [NUMBER/PERCENTAGE] are choosing it at purchase**

*Alternative headline, if the data is thin:*
**What UAE residents should check before freezing a health insurance premium for five years**

## Standfirst

Premium Lock has been on sale in Dubai and the Northern Emirates since June. It holds the base premium flat for up to five years for an extra 7 per cent a year, and it ends the moment a policyholder makes an inpatient claim.

## Lead

Since June, health insurance customers in Dubai and the Northern Emirates have been able to hold their base premium at the rate they signed up on for up to five years, through an add-on called Premium Lock sold on Policybazaar.ae. [NUMBER OR PERCENTAGE — CONFIRM WITH PRODUCT] customers have added it since launch.

The add-on costs 7 per cent of the base plan premium a year. On a plan priced at AED 3,500, that is AED 245, taking the annual outlay to AED 3,745 and holding it there for up to five years or until the first inpatient hospitalisation claim, whichever comes first.

## Why it matters

Medical inflation in the UAE runs between 5 and 10 per cent a year. On top of that, insurers apply age-band loading, which can add 20 to 25 per cent to a premium the year a customer crosses a milestone birthday. Policybazaar.ae data showed that close to 40 per cent of customers who saw a premium increase at renewal had not made a single claim and had no pre-existing conditions. [CONFIRM THIS FIGURE IS STILL CURRENT AS OF AUGUST 2026]

## How does Premium Lock work?

Health insurance premiums are recalculated at every renewal. Premium Lock fixes the base premium at the level a customer bought on and holds it for up to five years from the purchase date.

The benefit is powered by Care Health Insurance and sold on plans from two UAE insurers: Dubai Insurance Company (DIC) and HAYAH Insurance Company PJSC, both licensed by the Central Bank of the UAE. It is available on eight DIC plans, including Dubai Care Standard and Next Care Standard, and on two HAYAH plans, MedNet Standard and MedNet NRI Care.

Premium Lock is sold only through Policybazaar.ae.

## How much does Premium Lock cost?

Seven per cent of the base plan premium, charged every year alongside the premium.

| Base annual premium | Premium Lock cost | Total held for up to 5 years |
|---|---|---|
| AED 2,000 | AED 140 | AED 2,140 |
| AED 3,500 | AED 245 | AED 3,745 |
| AED 6,000 | AED 420 | AED 6,420 |

By comparison, a customer moving into a higher age band could otherwise see roughly AED [FIGURE — CONFIRM WITH PRODUCT] added at a single renewal on an AED 3,500 plan.

## Who is eligible for Premium Lock?

- Residents of Dubai and the Northern Emirates
- Adults up to age 60. It is not sold to anyone above 60
- Customers who declare no pre-existing medical conditions at purchase. If any member of the policy declares one, the policy is ineligible
- Standalone child policies are excluded

## What ends the Premium Lock benefit?

The lock is not an unconditional five-year freeze. It ends on any of the following:

- An inpatient hospitalisation claim
- A maternity-related outpatient claim
- An oncology or cancer-related outpatient claim
- An upgrade to a higher plan category, or a switch to another product line
- A regulatory change that introduces mandatory benefits carrying additional cost

If an undeclared pre-existing condition comes to light after purchase, the benefit is removed immediately and the add-on fee is not refunded.

## Is Premium Lock available in Abu Dhabi?

No. It is currently sold to residents of Dubai and the Northern Emirates only.

## Can Premium Lock be added at renewal, or only at purchase?

[CONFIRM WITH PRODUCT BEFORE PUBLISHING. Do not guess this. If the answer is purchase-only, say so plainly: "The add-on can only be selected when the policy is bought, not added to a policy already in force."]

## What should customers check before opting in?

Three things decide whether the 7 per cent is worth paying: how close the customer is to an age band that would push the premium up, how likely an inpatient claim is in the next five years, and whether the eligible DIC or HAYAH plan matches the cover they actually need. A customer who expects a hospital admission will lose the lock on that claim and will have paid the add-on fee for nothing.

# PART B — Format rules, keyed to the sample

## Structure rules

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Question-form H2s that contain the search term | "How much does Premium Lock cost?" gets pulled into Google AI Overviews and ChatGPT answers. "What does the feature offer?" gets pulled into nothing. |
| Answer in the first sentence under each H2 | AI answer engines lift the sentence directly after the heading. Bury the answer in sentence three and it is not liftable. |
| One fact per bullet in the conditions lists | Prose paragraphs listing five conditions get summarised into one. Bullets survive. |
| A cost table, not just a percentage | "7 per cent" is abstract. AED 245 on AED 3,500 is concrete and quotable. Desks reuse tables. |
| Fan-out questions get their own H2 | "Is Premium Lock available in Abu Dhabi?" is what a reader will actually search after reading. Answering it in the piece keeps them on the page. |
| Target 750 to 900 words | The current draft was 490 words with the newsworthy specifics missing. Below 700 there is not enough substance for a business desk. |

## Language rules

**Every number needs a worked example.** "7 per cent of the base plan premium" alone is not enough. Show the arithmetic.

**Name entities in full on first mention.** "DIC" means nothing to a reader. Write "Dubai Insurance Company (DIC)". Same for "HAYAH Insurance Company PJSC" and "Care Health Insurance".

**State the geography every time availability comes up.** Gulf News is a national title with heavy Abu Dhabi readership. A release that says "available on select plans" without saying Dubai and Northern Emirates only is misleading to a large share of readers, and that is a compliance problem, not a style one.

**Use the hedge once, precisely.** The previous draft said "subject to the terms and conditions of the policy" or "subject to the policy terms" three times. Repeating a vague caveat is worse than stating the actual limit once. Replace hedging with the specific condition: "the lock ends on the first inpatient claim".

**Avoid these phrases outright:**

| Do not write | Write instead |
|---|---|
| "The amount a customer may save will depend on the premium, age and policy selected." | The worked example from the cost table. |
| "another factor to consider alongside coverage, exclusions, insurer terms and the overall premium" | A closing fact: who it is sold to, and who it is not sold to. |
| "subject to the terms and conditions of the policy" (repeated) | The named condition itself. |
| "This means customers need to look at the policy terms carefully." | What specifically to look at, and why. |
| "delighted", "excited", "testament to", "committed to", "pivotal", "underscores" | Plain statement of what the thing does. |

**No em dashes.** Use commas, full stops or colons.

**Sentence length should vary.** The previous draft ran almost every sentence between 18 and 28 words, which reads as machine-generated. Mix short declaratives with longer ones.

## Accuracy rules

Every one of these was missing or incomplete in the previous draft. None is optional.

| Must appear | Verified against |
|---|---|
| Dubai and Northern Emirates only | Policybazaar.ae Premium Lock product page |
| Age 60 upper limit | Same |
| All five voiding events, not just inpatient claims | Same. Maternity and oncology OPD claims void the lock, and this matters most to the readers most likely to buy. |
| "Up to five years **or** until the first inpatient claim, whichever comes first" | Same. "Up to five years" on its own overstates the benefit. |
| Standalone child policies excluded | Same |
| No refund if an undeclared condition is found | Same |
| Named executive quote with exact title | Newsdesk requirement, and quoted attributed statements get cited by AI engines far more often |
| Disclaimer plus broker licence line | YMYL financial content published under our name |

Do not print any figure that is not either on our own product page or confirmed in writing by Product. If a number cannot be confirmed, cut the sentence rather than softening it.

# PART D — Alternative hooks, if the primary data is unavailable

**Fallback Hook 1 — renewal season.** Peak health insurance renewal months in the UAE, framed around what a resident facing a renewal increase can and cannot do about it. Premium Lock is one of the options covered rather than the subject. Needs: renewal-volume seasonality data.

**Fallback Hook 2 — consumer explainer.** "What UAE residents should check before freezing a health insurance premium for five years." Leads on the conditions, not the benefit. This works without any new data and is the safest option, because the June coverage did not explain what voids the lock. Use the Alternative headline in Part A and reorder so "What ends the Premium Lock benefit?" comes directly after the Lead.

**Fallback Hook 3 — market context.** UAE medical inflation against regional or global figures, with premium-guarantee products as the market response. Needs: a citable third-party inflation source such as an Aon or WTW medical trend report.
