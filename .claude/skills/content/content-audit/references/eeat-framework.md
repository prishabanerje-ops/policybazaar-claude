# E-E-A-T Framework — UAE Health & Travel Insurance / YMYL Content

Scoring rubric for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness,
covering Policybazaar UAE health insurance and travel insurance content. Read
`references/policybazaar-context.md` first — it has the specific regulator
names, terminology, and credential patterns each example below draws on.

---

## FILE MODE vs URL MODE — What to Check

**FILE MODE (draft / .md / .docx / pasted text):**
The writer controls the content body, not the publishing metadata.
Do NOT check or penalise for: author name, credentials, byline, publish date,
last-updated date, regulator citation links, license signal, brand page signals.
These are added at publishing time, not by the content writer.

Score E-E-A-T in FILE MODE only on what the writer controls:
- Does the *writing itself* demonstrate firsthand experience? (specific examples, not generic)
- Is the technical detail accurate and current? (expertise in the words, not the byline)
- Is content balanced and not misleading? (trustworthiness in the claims)
- Does the content align with regulatory/consensus reality? (authoritativeness in substance)

**URL MODE (live page):**
Check all E-E-A-T signals as defined in the full rubric below — author, credentials,
dates, citations, and license signals are live on the page and fully auditable.

---

Both health and travel insurance content are YMYL (Your Money or Your Life). Google
and AI engines apply the strictest E-E-A-T scrutiny to YMYL content. A page that fails
E-E-A-T on insurance topics will struggle to rank AND struggle to be cited by
AI engines.

---

## Why YMYL Matters Here

**Health insurance:** buying decisions directly impact financial and physical
health outcomes — underinsurance, wrong deductible/premium choices, or missing DHA/DOH
mandatory-coverage requirements all carry real consequences.

**Travel insurance:** coverage decisions directly impact financial outcomes and safety
abroad — inadequate medical cover for the destination, policies that don't satisfy visa
requirements, or missed claim conditions all carry real consequences.

AI engines know this. They heavily weight YMYL content on E-E-A-T signals. A page
without author credentials, sourced claims, or institutional affiliation has a low
chance of being cited in an AI Overview or ChatGPT response — regardless of SEO.

## E — Experience (0–25 pts in E-E-A-T subscore)

**What it means:** Does the content show *first-hand* or lived experience with the
subject? Not just knowledge of general principles, but real UAE-specific familiarity.

**Signals to look for — Health:**
- References to real UAE insurer names, specific plan tiers, actual DHA/DOH requirements
- Practical examples only someone familiar with UAE insurance would write
  (e.g., "most network hospitals in Dubai require pre-authorization for specialist visits")
- Comparison based on actual platform data ("based on our comparison of 40+ plans")
- Acknowledgment of known UAE pain points (waiting periods, pre-existing exclusions)

**Signals to look for — Travel:**
- References to real UAE insurers' travel products, named plan tiers, actual visa
  coverage requirements for common destinations from the UAE
- Practical examples only someone familiar with UAE outbound travel would write
  (e.g., claim documentation realities, how UAE residents' visa categories affect
  requirements)
- Comparison based on actual platform data ("based on our comparison of X travel plans")
- Acknowledgment of known pain points (pre-existing condition exclusions, claim deadlines,
  per-item baggage limits)

**Flags for low Experience (either vertical):**
- Generic content that could apply to any country's market
- No UAE-specific nuance
- Claims any informed person could make without real industry access

**Scoring:**
| Signals | Points |
|---|---|
| 3+ UAE-specific experience signals, data from real platform usage | 20–25 |
| 1–2 specific signals | 10–19 |
| Vague experience, could be any market | 1–9 |
| No experience signals | 0 |

## E — Expertise (0–25 pts in E-E-A-T subscore)

**What it means:** Does the content demonstrate subject-matter expertise — accurate,
technically correct knowledge of the vertical's concepts and UAE regulations?

**Signals to look for — Health:**
- Author attribution with relevant credentials (e.g., "Certified Insurance Advisor",
  "DHA/DOH-familiar reviewer")
- Accurate technical terminology (deductible, co-pay, co-insurance, network,
  pre-authorization, open enrollment)
- Correct regulatory references (DHA for Dubai, DOH for Abu Dhabi — not the outdated HAAD)
- Accurate mandatory coverage thresholds

**Signals to look for — Travel:**
- Author attribution with relevant credentials (insurance advisory or travel-industry
  background)
- Accurate technical terminology (single-trip vs. annual multi-trip, policy excess,
  territorial validity, trip curtailment vs. cancellation, repatriation)
- Correct visa/coverage references attributed to the right authority (consulate/embassy
  guidance for visa minimums, the insurer's policy wording for coverage terms)
- Accurate coverage limits and premiums for named products

**Flags for low Expertise (either vertical):**
- Regulatory body names wrong or confused (e.g., HAAD is now DOH)
- Generic definitions that misrepresent how the product actually works
- No author name — "The Team" without individual attribution
- Expertise claimed but not demonstrated (just says "our experts recommend")

**Scoring:**
| Signals | Points |
|---|---|
| Named author with credentials + accurate technical content + correct regulatory refs | 20–25 |
| Accurate technical content but no named author | 10–19 |
| Some expertise signals but errors or vague credentials | 5–9 |
| Generic, no author, inaccuracies | 0–4 |

## A — Authoritativeness (0–25 pts in E-E-A-T subscore)

**What it means:** Is the source recognized as authoritative on this topic? Are external
signals present that confirm authority?

**Signals to look for — Health:**
- Explicit identification as a licensed insurance aggregator/broker in UAE
- Reference to regulatory compliance (UAE Insurance Authority, DHA approval)
- Authoritative external citations: DHA.gov.ae, DOH.gov.ae, MOHAP.gov.ae
- Statistics or data cited with source attribution; expert quotes from named advisors

**Signals to look for — Travel:**
- Explicit identification as a licensed insurance aggregator/broker in UAE
- Authoritative external citations: official consulate/embassy pages for visa
  requirements, named insurers' policy wordings for coverage claims
- Statistics or data cited with source attribution; expert quotes from named advisors

**Flags for low Authoritativeness (either vertical):**
- No brand identification (anonymous source)
- No regulatory/license signal
- Claims made without any source attribution
- Cites competitors or aggregators as sources (credibility dilution)

**Scoring:**
| Signals | Points |
|---|---|
| Licensed platform identified + regulatory citations + sourced data + expert quotes | 20–25 |
| Brand present + 1–2 authority signals | 10–19 |
| Brand present but no authority signals | 5–9 |
| Anonymous content, no authority | 0–4 |

## T — Trustworthiness (0–25 pts in E-E-A-T subscore)

**What it means:** Can readers trust the accuracy, transparency, and intentions of this
content? This is where content quality meets editorial integrity.

**Signals to look for (both verticals):**
- Last-updated date visible (premiums, plans, and regulations all change)
- Clear disclosure of commercial relationship (e.g., "we earn commission from partner
  insurers")
- Balanced coverage — not just promoting high-commission plans
- Accurate claims (no exaggerated benefits, no misleading omissions of exclusions,
  waiting periods, or policy excess)
- No dark patterns in CTAs (no fake urgency for products that are always available)
- Contact information or support channel referenced

**Flags for low Trustworthiness (either vertical):**
- No publication or update date (especially on regulatory/premium content)
- Missing or buried disclosure of commercial relationship
- Exaggerated claims ("best insurance in UAE" without evidence)
- Misleading or omitted exclusions, claim conditions, or policy excess
- Fake urgency or scarcity

**Scoring:**
| Signals | Points |
|---|---|
| Updated date + commercial disclosure + balanced coverage + accurate claims | 20–25 |
| 2–3 trust signals | 10–19 |
| 1 trust signal | 5–9 |
| No trust signals, or active red flags (misleading claims) | 0–4 |

## E-E-A-T Total Score: 0–100

Sum of four pillars. Used as an input to the GEO Parameter 2 scoring.

| Score | Rating |
|---|---|
| 80–100 | Strong E-E-A-T — citation-ready for AI engines |
| 60–79 | Moderate E-E-A-T — improvable with author + source additions |
| 40–59 | Weak E-E-A-T — high risk of being deprioritized by AI engines |
| 0–39 | Critical E-E-A-T gaps — content likely invisible to AI citation |

## Quick Fix Reference — Most Common E-E-A-T Gaps

| Gap | Fix |
|---|---|
| No author name | Add named author with title/credentials in byline |
| No update date | Add "Last updated: [Month Year]" near title |
| "The team" authorship | Replace with individual contributor + credential |
| Regulator confusion (e.g., HAAD instead of DOH) | Audit all regulatory body names against `references/policybazaar-context.md` |
| No source citations | Add links to the regulator's own site for regulatory claims |
| No commercial disclosure | Add a one-line disclosure near the top |
| Generic product descriptions | Replace with named plans, actual premium/coverage ranges |
| Missing exclusions or claim conditions | Add a section covering what's typically not included |
