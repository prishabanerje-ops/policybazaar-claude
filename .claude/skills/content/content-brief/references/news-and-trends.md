# News & Trends — Sourcing Current Hooks

A brief that cites something genuinely current — a rate change, a new regulation, a
seasonal pattern — is stronger than one built only on evergreen gaps. Use this during
Step 1 (research) and Step 2 (the Angle), not as a mandatory extra step: if nothing
current and relevant turns up, skip it rather than forcing a stretch.

---

## Where to Look

Prioritize, in this order:
1. **The relevant regulator directly** — DHA/DOH/MOHAP press pages for Policybazaar
   topics; Central Bank of the UAE announcements for Paisabazaar topics. A cited
   regulatory change is the strongest possible current-hook.
2. **UAE business/finance press** — Gulf News, Khaleej Times, The National, Zawya,
   Arabian Business. Search these by name alongside the topic (e.g.,
   `"Gulf News" DHA health insurance 2026`) rather than a generic web search, since
   generic results skew toward aggregator/SEO content over reporting.
3. **Bank/insurer own announcements** — rate changes, new product launches, seasonal
   promotions (e.g., Ramadan loan offers, back-to-school insurance bundles).

## Using What You Find

- A current regulatory or rate change belongs in the **Facts & Figures** table with its
  source, not just narrative color.
- A genuinely timely angle (a change that hasn't been widely covered yet, or one that
  competitors' existing pages predate) can sharpen **The Angle** — "competitor pages
  predate [recent change] and still cite the old figures" is a strong, concrete gap.
- Don't manufacture urgency. If the most current relevant fact is a year old and stable,
  say so plainly rather than searching until something looks new.

Follow the same sourcing discipline as every other research step in these skills:
paraphrase what you find, cite by source name, and never fabricate a headline, a
regulation, or a date.
