Executive Summary

  • The page advertises 18 business products and explains none of them. Every product tile is a label plus a "Get Quotes" button. There is no definition, no coverage detail, no exclusion, no price, and no claim process for any of the 18 lines.
  • None of the 18 product tiles contains a crawlable link to its sub-product page. The tiles are JS buttons with no href. Six live money pages (Professional Indemnity, Workers Compensation, Marine, D&O, Product Liability, Commercial General Liability) receive a link from the global footer only, not from their own topical parent.
  • No FAQ schema, no comparison table, no numbered process markup, no price anchor. All four AEO structured formats are absent or unmarked, and the page opens with navigation and tile labels rather than a definition, scoring 0 on answer-first.
  • Structured-data integrity problem. An Organization node named "Insurance Reviews" carries aggregateRating with reviewCount: "6" while the visible page states 43,419 reviews. Self-serving review markup about the business on its own page is outside Google's review-snippet eligibility, and the count mismatch is an independent risk.
  • YMYL trust signals are thin for a page in a regulated financial vertical. No named author, no reviewer, no last-updated date, no "not financial advice" disclaimer, no reference to the governing UAE insurance statute. The regulatory footer (CBUAE registration, DED trade licence, DHA permit, EIF number) is the page's strongest trust asset and it sits below everything.
  • The competitive bar is low, which is the opportunity. None of insurancemarket.ae, aig.ae or tmnf.ae has FAQ schema, a comparison table, a price anchor, an author byline or an update date either. AIG actively cites a repealed statute (Federal Law No. 6 of 2007). First mover on depth and trust markup takes this vertical.
  • Technical hygiene is genuinely good. HTTP 200, HSTS, CSP, self-referencing canonical, clean URL, correct viewport, JSON-LD only with zero microdata, 71 images all carrying alt text, dedicated per-product XML sitemaps. The problem is content and internal linking, not infrastructure.
Top Priority Action Make the 18 product tiles real anchor links to their existing sub-pages, then add a 150-word explainer plus an indicative price band under each of the top six lines. All 11 sub-product URLs tested return HTTP 200 and are present in dedicated XML sitemaps, so the destination pages already exist. Today the hub advertises the full portfolio while passing internal link equity to almost none of it, and gives a reader or an AI crawler nothing quotable about any product.

Quick Wins — implement in < 1 week
1. Turn all 18 product tiles into real anchor links What: wrap each tile in <a href="/business-insurance/[slug]/"> pointing to its existing sub-page, keeping "Get Quotes" as a secondary button inside the card.
Why: eleven sub-pages tested at HTTP 200 and five of them currently receive no link from their own topical parent. A JS button passes no equity and gives a crawler no path.

Why: H1 → H3 with no H2 is a broken document outline, and the tile block is the largest single content region on the page.
How: add an H2 above the tile carousel, for example "Types of Business Insurance in the UAE", and keep the tiles as H3s beneath it. That H2 also captures a real query that gargashinsurance.com and seveninsurancebrokers.com currently rank for.
